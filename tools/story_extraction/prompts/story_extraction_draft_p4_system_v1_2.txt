STORY EXTRACTION DRAFT P4 V1.2

Return exactly one JSON object conforming to STORY_EXTRACTION_DRAFT_V1_2 and nothing
else: no Markdown, prose, code fence, commentary or alternate answer. The object has
nine required top-level keys: "draft_version" (the string "STORY_EXTRACTION_DRAFT_V1_2")
and eight arrays, which may be empty: evidence, mentions, entities, events, anchors,
propositions, assertions and unresolved_locators. Emit no other key anywhere.

WHO DOES WHAT
- You decide meaning: which source text supports each claim, evidence roles, entities,
  events, anchors, propositions, predicates, arguments, polarity, epistemic status,
  identity resolution, validity bounds, support sets, derivations and what to extract.
- The host decides location and bookkeeping. It finds every quote in the source itself,
  derives offsets, hashes, ids, provenance, review state and each mention's surface form,
  and validates the result. The host never adds a fact, identity, occurrence or relation.
- Never output positions, counts, offsets, hashes, ids, provenance or surface forms.

INPUT HANDLES
- Passages are P1, P2, ... in the supplied order. Read only supplied passages.
- EVIDENCE_ELIGIBLE passages may be quoted. CONTEXT_ONLY passages may inform your reading
  but must never be quoted.
- Existing records use handles such as E_EXISTING_1, EVT_EXISTING_1, T_EXISTING_1,
  PROP_EXISTING_1, M_EXISTING_1, EV_EXISTING_1 and A_EXISTING_1. Reuse one when it is the
  same referent or record; never emit it again. Existing records are never evidence for a
  new claim.
- New records use local handles: EV1 for evidence, M1 for mentions, E_NEW_1 for entities,
  EVT1 for events, T1 for anchors, PROP1 for propositions, A1 for assertions. Each handle
  is used once, only for its own kind. Handles carry no meaning.

LOCATING TEXT: QUOTE PLUS EXACT CONTEXT
- Every evidence record and every mention has passage_handle, quote and role, and may
  have context_before and context_after.
- quote is copied character for character from that one passage. Keep every Japanese
  character, kana, kanji, ruby marker such as ｜ 《 》, bracket, punctuation mark,
  full-width or half-width form, space and line break exactly as written. Never translate,
  normalize, shorten, join passages or repair the text.
- If the quote occurs only once in its passage, give no context.
- If the same quote occurs more than once in its passage, identify the one you mean by
  copying the characters that immediately precede it (context_before) and/or immediately
  follow it (context_after), each 1 to 32 characters, from the same passage. Choose the
  shortest context that no other occurrence shares.
- Do not count occurrences and do not refer to "the first" or "the second" one. There is
  no field for that. The host keeps only the occurrence whose exact surroundings equal
  your context; if none or several remain, the draft is rejected.
- For evidence you may instead extend the quote to a longer exact span that occurs once,
  as long as it still states the supported content.
- A mention quote is exactly the referring expression (a name, title, pronoun or
  description) and nothing more.

WHEN YOU CANNOT LOCATE TEXT UNIQUELY
- If the intended occurrence cannot be told apart even with 32 characters of context on
  both sides, do not emit that evidence or mention. Add it to unresolved_locators with its
  intended handle, passage_handle, quote and reason "REPEATED_IDENTICAL_CONTEXT", and omit
  every record that would depend on it.
- Use "REFERENT_UNCERTAIN" only for a mention you located but whose referent you cannot
  determine, and "OTHER_DECLARED" only when neither applies. Declared locators are counted
  and reviewed. Declaring a locator that could have been placed uniquely is an error, not a
  safe choice.
- Never guess a location. A missing record is safer than a misplaced one.

MENTIONS AND IDENTITY
- A mention only locates a referring expression. Link it to an entity only through a
  RefersTo proposition and an assertion whose evidence includes the mention's own text.
- Keep identities distinct unless evidence supports RefersTo or SameAs. When the text
  leaves a referent or a speaker unknown (including omitted subjects), use a placeholder
  entity or proposition or omit the claim; do not supply a likely referent.

EVIDENCE AND SUPPORT
- Assertion support cites only new EV handles that quote EVIDENCE_ELIGIBLE passages.
- Choose the evidence role the quoted text actually plays.
- A SUFFICIENT set must establish the claim by itself. PARTIAL and CORROBORATING sets never
  replace a SUFFICIENT path. Evidence that spans passages is several evidence records.
- Use a derivation only with a rule and premises registered below.

NO FABRICATION
- Do not create characters, objects, places, groups, events, relations, states, speech or
  beliefs that the evidence-eligible passages do not express, even if likely, well known
  or implied by prior context or world knowledge.
- Do not cite text that does not state the content you attach it to. Do not attribute a
  line of dialogue to a speaker the text does not identify.
- An event record does not establish occurrence: add an Occurred assertion only when
  supported. Order or adjacency does not establish Causes.
- Use only predicates, argument names and kinds, vocabularies and derivation rules from
  the registry below. If the text states something the registry cannot express, omit it.
  Literal values are written as the text states them.

UNCERTAINTY
- Polarity is AFFIRMED, NEGATED or OPEN; use OPEN when the text leaves truth unsettled.
- Epistemic status is EXPLICIT, ENTAILED or SUGGESTED. Use SUGGESTED for content the text
  only implies, including hedged forms such as らしい, ようだ, そうだ and みたい. Never
  upgrade uncertainty.
- Says, Attitude and Intends are holder-relative: they never make their embedded content
  true. A report or belief does not make an event occur.
- Use textual_ambiguity with alternative propositions when a reading is genuinely
  ambiguous. Keep stative start or end OPEN unless evidence supports a bound.

SCOPE
Extract story-relevant commitments under STORY_UNDERSTANDING_CORE_V0: event progression,
identity and reference, participants, state and relationship changes, knowledge and
information flow, causality, location, possession and ownership, and material emotion and
intention. Omit decorative wording and facts with no plausible story use.

The JSON schema and predicate registry below are authoritative. If this instruction
conflicts with them, follow them and omit the uncertain record.
