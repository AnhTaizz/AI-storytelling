# H2 Comparative Review

## Research Question
Does separating factual story understanding from creative script generation improve factual fidelity while preserving the narrative advantages observed in RUN_0002?

## Experimental Design
- **RUN_0002**: Direct Prompt → Vietnamese Script
- **RUN_0003**: Japanese Source → Story Brief (Japanese) → Vietnamese Script

## Methodology Limitations
Agent-runtime execution was used. The declared generation contexts were instructed but were not independently machine-enforced. The generated Story Brief contains at least one source-negative constraint (do not assume Amane's room is messy) resembling a prior-run failure pattern, so prior-experiment influence cannot be independently ruled out.

## Story Brief Fidelity
The Story Brief successfully established a comparatively strong factual boundary in this run, preserving key details like Mahiru's sitting posture, her hair color, and the ambiguity of her motives (marked as `UNKNOWN`).

## Story Brief Representation Weaknesses
The extraction process struggled to maintain epistemic markers. For example, it flattened hearsay (`らしい` regarding Mahiru's athletic ability) into explicit fact, and failed to strictly tag supported inferences (like the time of day) versus direct explicit facts.

## Script Fidelity to Story Brief
The Script failed to strictly adhere to the Story Brief. The Stage B Writer introduced new factual and interpretive drift that was NOT present in the Story Brief.

## RUN_0002 vs RUN_0003 Factual Comparison
- **Hair Color**: RUN_0002 drifted to `hạt dẻ` (chestnut). RUN_0003 correctly used `hạt lanh` (flaxen), derived from the Story Brief.
- **Ending**: RUN_0002 explicitly predicted future events. RUN_0003 returned to a more subtle implication, matching the Story Brief.
- **Scene State**: RUN_0003 correctly maintained Mahiru sitting on the swing.

## Narrative Comparison
- **RUN_0002** was punchy, highly engaging, and moved quickly.
- **RUN_0003** maintains a conversational and engaging tone (humor, commentary), but suffers from severe pacing issues. The exposition is heavily padded, taking much longer to reach the inciting incident.

## Length / Compression
- **Target**: 900–1100 words
- **RUN_0002**: 1045 words (Passed)
- **RUN_0003**: 1648 words (Failed)
The Script Writer completely failed the compression contract, elaborating excessively on Amane's internal state and relationships.

## Output Integrity
No Cyrillic or Bengali contamination was mechanically detected in RUN_0003. However, this may be stochastic variation rather than a direct result of the Story Brief.

## Failure Attribution
| Issue               | Story Brief  | Final Script | Likely Failure Layer |
| ------------------- | ------------ | ------------ | -------------------- |
| Hair color          | Correct      | Correct      | Improved             |
| Mahiru rain reason  | UNKNOWN      | Maintained   | Improved             |
| Scene state         | Correct      | Correct      | Improved             |
| Future implication  | Conservative | Conservative | Improved             |
| Amane "knows his place" | Absent   | Invented     | Writer adherence     |
| 1648 words          | N/A          | Failed       | Narrative generation |

## Benefits Observed
The Story Brief pipeline restored specific fidelity details (hair color, physical state) and successfully captured ambiguity (`UNKNOWN`).

## Regressions Observed
Severe length and compression regression. The Writer compensated for the dry input by inventing excessive internal monologue and over-elaborating stylistic expressions ("búp bê sống", "buổi chiều định mệnh").

## H2 Verdict
**PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED**

## Architecture Implications
The truth representation and final script have different failure profiles. A separate factual representation is empirically useful, but the Story Brief alone does NOT reliably constrain the Writer. 

## Recommended Next Experiment
**H6 — Factual Script Critic / Validation Loop**
Because the Writer layer introduces drift despite a clean Story Brief, testing a post-generation factual critic is motivated to see if it can detect and/or reduce Writer deviations from the Story Brief.
