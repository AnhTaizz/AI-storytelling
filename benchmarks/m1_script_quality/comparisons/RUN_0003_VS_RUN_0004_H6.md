# H6 Comparative Review

## Research Question
Can a post-generation critic using the Story Brief as the declared factual boundary detect Writer deviations and, when its findings are supplied to a controlled revision stage, reduce those deviations without unacceptable narrative degradation?

## Experimental Design
- RUN_0003 Story Brief and original script were reused.
- The script was passed to the Factual Critic (V1).
- The Critic produced a report (`critic_report.yaml`).
- The report and original script were passed to a Critic-Guided Revision stage.
- Target word count was 900–1100.

## Controlled Inputs
- RUN_0003 `story_brief.yaml`
- RUN_0003 `script.md`
- Raw Japanese source was NOT provided to Critic or Revision.
- Previous failure reports were NOT provided.

## Methodology Limitations
Agent-runtime declared contexts were instructed but not independently machine-enforced. No formal gold-standard annotations exist, so detection precision/recall is qualitatively estimated.

## Mechanical Comparison
- RUN_0003 measured word count: ~1565 words (Manifest: 1648).
- RUN_0004 measured word count: ~1021 words (Manifest: 1074).
- Reduction: ~544 words (~34.8%).
- The revision successfully brought the script within the 900–1100 target range.

## Critic Detection Accuracy
- The Critic accurately identified two high-impact internal state deviations (V1: Amane's sense of inferiority; V2: Amane losing sleep). Both were categorized as TRUE_POSITIVE.
- It also correctly diagnosed structural compression needs.

## Critic Misses
The Critic missed several factual/interpretive deviations:
- "khu công viên nhỏ" (Physical detail absent from Brief).
- "nhún vai" (Unsupported physical action).
- "ngồi co ro" (Unsupported physical state).
- Converting the possibility of catching a cold into certainty (Stronger than Brief).
- Amane's invented internal resolution that he could leave "mà không thấy tội lỗi nữa."

## Revision Effectiveness
- V1 (inferiority motive) was **RESOLVED**; the language was removed entirely.
- V2 (losing sleep) was **RESOLVED**; softened to "lương tâm sẽ vô cùng cắn rứt" (conscience would bite him), which better aligns with the Brief.
- Redundant exposition in the first half was heavily compressed.

## Revision-Introduced Drift
The revision stayed generally faithful to the source RUN_0003 and Story Brief. One minor addition was "trời đổ mưa tầm tã sau giờ học" (after school), which was actually more faithful to the Story Brief ("放課後または夕方") than the original script. A narrator sign-off was added at the end ("có lẽ chúng ta đành phải chờ xem..."). No major harmful factual drift was introduced by the revision.

## Remaining Factual Drift
Since the Critic missed several deviations, they remained in the revised script:
- "khu công viên nhỏ"
- "nhún vai"
- "ngồi co ro"
- Certainty of catching a cold
- "không thấy tội lỗi nữa"

## Compression Comparison
The H6 critic-guided revision pipeline achieved target-length compliance in this run. Most of the compression occurred by trimming repetitive exposition about Mahiru's beauty and Amane's desire for detachment before the inciting incident.

## Narrative Quality Comparison
- The hook is cleaner and pacing to the inciting incident is much faster.
- The overall storytelling quality, flow, and Vietnamese phrasing remained natural and engaging, not robotic.
- Some creator commentary and stylistic flair were preserved, though slightly reduced due to compression.
- Emotional flow is intact, and important story beats (rain scene, umbrella handoff, unknown motive) were preserved without over-compression.

## Comparison With RUN_0002
- Hook and pacing are competitive with RUN_0002, perhaps slightly better due to faster progression.
- Factual fidelity is higher than RUN_0002 due to the removal of major invented motives, though some minor drift remains.
- Word count compliance is strictly better.

## Output Integrity
No obvious unexpected foreign-script contamination mechanically detected.

## Benefits Observed
- Effectively resolved major internal motive inventions.
- Excellent structural compression to meet length targets while preserving narrative flow.

## Remaining Failures
- Critic coverage is sparse, missing claim-level details.
- Minor factual/staging drift remains untouched.

## H6 Verdict
**PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED**. The Critic successfully detected high-impact deviations and the Revision resolved them without narrative degradation, while achieving target compression. However, the Critic's coverage was incomplete, leaving several unsupported claims in the final script.

## Architecture Implications
- **Truth Boundary**: A separate Story Brief is empirically useful as a factual reference.
- **Critic Utility**: A semantic Critic provides useful, actionable information.
- **Critic Coverage**: Critic V1 coverage is insufficient for claim-by-claim fidelity.
- **Revision**: A one-pass revision provides useful improvement without degrading narrative quality.
- **Production Readiness**: Neither Critic V1 nor the one-pass Revision are production-ready.

## Recommended Next Hypothesis
**H6b — Claim-Level Factual Critic**
Hypothesis: Requiring the Critic to enumerate and classify meaningful factual/interpretive claims in the script before issuing violations may improve detection coverage compared with the sparse free-form Critic V1.
